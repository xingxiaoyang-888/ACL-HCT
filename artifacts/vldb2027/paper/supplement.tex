\section{Exact Computation and RAM Reuse}
This figure retains the registered exact/RAM endpoints at $q=1,10,100$; the main paper additionally displays the same accounting formulas over $1\leq q\leq100$. Both use the recorded timing medians, not additional repeated-workload experiments. Format setup and full-output cache construction, including device-to-host transfer, are paid before RAM gathering. Common graph/checkpoint loading remains separately scoped. A fixed graph and model are required; changes invalidate the cache. In particular, a cached full-graph output is not a free hidden representation or a teacher input to the sampled predictor.

The arxiv/SAGE mean paid RAM time is 0.191 seconds at $q=1$, compared with 0.184 seconds for exact recomputation, and 0.269 versus 0.445 seconds at $q=10$. GAT's $q=100$ paid times of 14.404, 1.102 and 1.026 seconds retain their seed-level variation and host-context limitation. The crossover is an analytic consequence of the recorded cost components, not a separately measured guarantee for a new workload. Main-paper memory comparisons are scoped to the displayed middle tier: a high-budget sampled GAT point can use more allocated GPU memory than exact computation.

The new round's allocated GPU use is 17,299 seconds: 269 for three PubMed base trainings, 311 for successful label-free calibration, 13 for the preserved metadata failure and 16,706 for final evaluation. All resources are closed. The metadata repair changed no model, sampler or numerical computation, and accepted qualifications were not repeated. Allocation time is not deployment latency. Nested scheduler, worker, controller and phase timings have different or overlapping scopes and are not added; missing child CPU observations are not replaced with zero. The final offline synthesis records 11.375 native CPU-seconds and 14.666 wall-seconds without added GPU computation.

The release supplies the 15 final-study checkpoints and 24 historical checkpoints, together with versioned result assets. Native dependencies are reconstructed from pinned upstream sources rather than redistributed as complete third-party trees.
