\subsection{Local restoration can miss an upstream error}
Table~\ref{tab:airport} reports the last-layer four-corner comparison for the original full-trained HyboNet/Airport checkpoints. Each seed uses 239 validation targets and 16 paired plans. Restoring only the local final aggregation changes mean accuracy from 79.5502\% to 80.2476\%. Restoring its incoming messages while retaining final-layer sampling instead reaches 93.5146\%, compared with 94.4212\% for the exact path. The joint interaction is approximately $0.2092$ percentage points using the displayed rounded means. These improvements must not be added as independent error-source proportions.

The error transitions reveal more than net gain. Across 11,472 node--plan events, local restoration corrects 132 errors and harms 52 previously correct predictions. Upstream restoration corrects 1,718 and harms 116; joint restoration corrects 1,781 and harms 75. For events whose final neighborhood is already complete, local restoration changes no prediction, whereas restoring incoming representations corrects 1,433 and harms 24. This is direct evidence of an inherited error in this specific computation. It does not identify which earlier layer caused it, or establish curvature as its cause.

An individual earlier-layer replacement can also hurt. Replacing the third layer's sampled aggregation by the full one reduces seed 23 accuracy by 0.8630 percentage points in the original layerwise experiment. Therefore, neither ``more exact at a layer'' nor its presumed reduction of local omission guarantees a monotone task improvement after the suffix.

\subsection{Training changes the reference and the opportunity}
On the separate common set of 476 Airport validation nodes, the original full-trained checkpoints score 93.4174\% under full inference and 78.0156\% under K8 inference. Matched-K8 training yields 88.7999\% under K8 and 89.9860\% under full inference. The difference between old full-trained full inference and new matched sampled inference is 4.6175 points, of which 1.1861 points is the within-new-checkpoint full-versus-sampled difference. The remaining 3.4314 points compares different weights under full inference. This is an arithmetic separation, not a causal allocation of training damage and sampling damage.

Matched training closes 70.0199\% of the original gap when computed as a ratio of the corresponding mean differences. This descriptive ratio does not transfer the old upstream oracle effect to the new checkpoints. Indeed, the matched-weight first-layer restoration changes mean accuracy by $-0.0569$ points. A paper reporting only the old large sampling shock would overstate the practical headroom after deployment-matched training.

\subsection{A broader collection contains exceptions}
Across the historical 24 checkpoints, mixing the two original training regimes, full accuracy has a macro mean of 79.1200\%. Low, middle, and high sampled-budget means are 72.0497\%, 75.2580\%, and 77.8201\%, respectively. At the high budget, however, HGCN/Disease-NC has sampled mean 64.9058\%, above its full reference of 64.6825\%. Sampling is not uniformly harmful in the available evidence. One recorded Disease checkpoint is degenerate and remains in the complete table rather than being replaced.

The new offline synthesis of the archived middle-budget fit captures supplies all four corners for all 24 checkpoints, with 16 original plans each; it executes no model and uses no new test labels. Figure~\ref{fig:taska} shows the HyboNet cells with every seed. On Airport, full-trained mean accuracies for corners 00, 10, 01, and 11 are 79.1141\%, 79.8407\%, 92.6383\%, and 93.4174\%. Matched-trained means are 88.8480\%, 89.3557\%, 89.4914\%, and 89.9860\%. Thus upstream restoration changes from $+13.5242$ points to $+0.6434$ points under the different training regime, while local restoration changes from $+0.7265$ to $+0.5077$ points. These are new summaries of \emph{old fit records}, not independent confirmation. Their 476-node targets and plans differ from the earlier A239 demonstration in Table~\ref{tab:airport}.

On Disease-NC, the corresponding full-trained means are 80.9524\%, 83.3829\%, 91.4187\%, and 95.2381\%. Matched-trained means are 76.1905\%, 77.2321\%, 77.8770\%, and 79.3651\%. Matched seed 11 is harmed by local restoration: 12 corrected versus 19 introduced errors over 672 node--plan events, a $-1.0417$-point change. Matched seed 37 benefits more from local than upstream restoration. Mean accuracy interactions are approximately $1.3889$ points for full training and $0.4464$ for matched training; the latter's per-seed range is $[-1.4881,1.9345]$ points. These effects cannot be interpreted as independent recovery shares.

Mean native margin and accuracy can also move in opposite directions. Local restoration on full-trained Airport seed 11 increases accuracy by 0.9585 points while its mean true-class margin decreases by 0.019628. On matched Disease-NC seed 37, the corresponding changes are $+3.2738$ points and $-0.053882$. These paired scalar observations illustrate why the average margin is not an accuracy surrogate; they do not establish that every node has a smaller representation error. Both native margins and local-estimator errors are compared within a named model/head and coordinate system. Their absolute scales across models cannot diagnose curvature.

For HGCN, $01=00$ and $11=10$ are structural aliases because the historical model has only one message layer. Its Disease-NC matched mean \emph{decreases} from 63.2937\% sampled to 62.6984\% with full local computation. Seed 11 has 11 corrections and 23 harms over 672 events ($-1.7857$ points), despite a $+0.007288$ mean-margin change; the degenerate seed 37 remains at 50\%. These outcomes remain in the complete figure and per-seed artifact. They cannot be interpreted as a matched curvature contrast with the deeper HyboNet.

Historical explanatory prediction tests also limit a stronger story. Adding geometric and operator features to features for propagation and task margins reduced held-out cross-entropy by 2.8669\% and 1.1289\% in two observation settings, below a prespecified 5\% criterion. The corresponding margin-MSE reductions, 1.9018\% and 1.7786\%, were below 10\%, and calibration criteria failed. These negative tests are not removed because a simpler descriptive mechanism is plausible. The new study does not refit those predictors or present them as validated general error estimators.

The previous bounded compute-allocation study additionally compared upstream spending and two-view inference. Its label-assisted batch selector had only a 0.062076-point mean opportunity over the frozen middle-cost LABOR baseline, below the prespecified 0.5-point development gate. No learned selector was fitted after that failure. The new paper can use the operational comparison, but must not describe this undeveloped selector as a deployed improvement.

\subsection{Preparation and reuse can reverse the apparent cost result}
For the historical 24-checkpoint small-graph experiments, all three finite budgets were slower end to end than the corresponding full inference when paid planning and transfers were included, even though prepared forward-only computation could be faster. This conclusion concerns those implementations and graph sizes; it is not a claim that graph sampling is generally slower. It demonstrates why forward-only timing is insufficient evidence of deployment efficiency.

On the historical GraphSAGE fit workload, full-output caching was substantially cheaper than repeatedly constructing exact target dependency cones (Table~\ref{tab:cache}). Both routes serve the same target workload, and the cached route includes recorded preparation and precomputation components. The result is from ordered same-process measurements with limited cache-state control. It supports explicit amortization accounting, not a universal cold-start speedup.

\subsection{Correction as an auxiliary mechanism check}
The historical same-information HyboNet test compares a two-view task-projected geometric correction, E1, with ordinary averaging of the same two native-logit views. E1 scores 89.5658\% and the ordinary mean 89.5965\%; the single base view scores 89.0275\%. E1 and the ordinary mean agree on 22,705 of 22,848 events (99.3741\%). Among 143 differing events, E1 corrects 61, harms 68, and leaves both predictions wrong in 14. A gain over one view thus did not establish added value over the matching two-view control.

A separate HGCN/arxiv correction confirmation gives mean K15 accuracy 71.2164\% for plain, 71.2880\% for the candidate, and 71.2220\% for its generic control. The candidate's changes against plain across seeds are $-0.0906$, $+0.1292$, and $+0.1762$ percentage points. Its mean request time increases from 118.174 to 155.623 ms, and allocated GPU memory from 303 to 1,019 MiB. This is a weak average gain with a negative seed and higher cost, not evidence for a generally useful repair. These are adapted checkpoints from a different round and are not substituted into the new plain-model comparison.

The earlier operator-specific E module on a WordNet development ranking task has conditional positive results, but it is a different operator, task, and training comparison. We preserve that case in the artifact without using it to claim that the new unified correction or the new sampler study succeeded. No additional correction training is part of this paper's supplementary work.
