\section{Experimental Protocol and Evidence Boundaries}
\label{sec:protocol}
\subsection{Assets and bounded comparison}
The completed study reuses the 24-checkpoint historical captures and 12 arxiv base checkpoints, and adds three PubMed base trainings. Arxiv uses the original plain HGCN, HyboNet, GraphSAGE and GAT models, before the later 50-epoch correction adaptations. Each new group retains seeds 11, 23 and 37; all 15 final cells are complete. Weights were fixed by role and provenance, not correction gains. The supplementary inventory records every checkpoint's hash, training source, selection rule, split and regime, separating historical fit evidence from final evaluation.

The 24-checkpoint collection crosses two hyperbolic implementations, two datasets, two training regimes, and three seeds. Airport uses budgets $4/8/16$; Disease-NC uses $1/2/4$, with full-neighborhood references. The historical HGCN implementation has one message-passing layer. HyboNet has five on Airport and three on Disease-NC. Disease-NC has only 42 validation nodes, compared with 476 on Airport. In contrast, the new arxiv HGCN and HyboNet checkpoints each have three message layers, as do the new ordinary-model checkpoints. In particular, the historical HGCN upstream branch is a structural alias; this is not an assumption about the new three-layer HGCN. Equal depth alone does not match the models' heads, normalization or training. These facts constrain interpretation rather than being changed to make model effects look uniform.

For the new arxiv study, the only samplers are Uniform and LABOR. All use the same target IDs, target ordering, batch specification, and task metrics. Three operating budgets were calibrated without labels; the major mechanism intervention was fixed at the final message-passing layer at the middle budget. Each sampler's actual probabilities, weighting, self loops, empty-neighborhood rule, and random namespaces were frozen in the source contract. Existing HGCN and HyboNet weights were reused for the required comparable frozen tests. The second dataset was chosen using checkpoint availability and documented graph differences, not which dataset previously produced favorable effects.

The final target workload contains all 48,603 arxiv test nodes and all 1,000 PubMed test nodes. With fixed batch size 128, one workload is 380 arxiv requests (379 full batches and a final batch of 91) or eight PubMed requests (seven full batches and a final batch of 104). Each of the four repeats uses the same complete target workload. Calibration instead uses the fixed development targets specified below; its request medians are not final-workload times.

\paragraph{Fixed budgets and repeats.}
Uniform uses the same fanout at each layer: $5/15/30$ on arxiv and $1/4/8$ on PubMed. For each model, one seed-11 label-free calibration uses eight fixed batches of at most 128 targets, three timed repeats and one paid warm-up. LABOR-0 uses no external sampling-probability input and zero importance-sampling iterations. Its arxiv candidate fanouts are 5, 8, 12, 15, 20, 24, 30, 36, 48, 64, and 80; PubMed uses 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 12, and 16. For each Uniform tier, the rule selects the largest LABOR fanout whose median over 24 request timings does not exceed the Uniform time; other seeds inherit that lock. If none qualifies, the tier has no feasible cost-matched LABOR point, and the minimum fanout's actual cost is still reported. No interpolation or new sampler repairs an infeasible tier. New evaluation uses four paired repeats per seed and tier. Near-equal accuracy means no seed is more than 0.2 percentage points below the comparison point; all actual differences remain visible.

All five label-free A800 cost locks are accepted (Table~\ref{tab:a800-locks}); no accuracy labels were evaluated for this selection. SAGE/arxiv's low-tier minimum LABOR point exceeds its fixed target by about 0.298\%, so that tier is explicitly infeasible under the no-over-target rule. It is neither recalibrated nor treated as an achieved equal-cost match. On PubMed, all three Uniform tiers select the same LABOR $K=6$ point: these are three comparisons against fixed Uniform endpoints, not three distinct LABOR operating points. Uniform $K=8$ is measured faster than $K=4$ on that workload. We retain this nonmonotonicity rather than smoothing the costs or repeating calibration to obtain monotone points. The final workload's actual costs and deviations must still be reported. These discrete label-free measurements establish frozen budgets, not a quality--cost benefit. Cross-dataset absolute times do not compare identical target workloads.

\paragraph{Diagnostic versus deployment computation.}
The middle-budget four-corner intervention uses frozen whole-graph layerwise sampling plans to construct each sampled prefix, followed by the last-layer intervention on the complete incoming node-state arrays. This preserves the semantics of restoring local computation without filling unvisited upstream states with zeros or exact representations. Deployment quality and cost instead use target dependency-cone requests. Their baselines are named separately and are not assumed to agree bitwise or to define the same sampling distribution. Whole-graph diagnostic state caches and exact references are paid diagnostic resources, not free deployment inputs. GAT retains native sampled-neighborhood softmax and exactly one self message; a new inverse-probability attention correction is not introduced, and its output is not claimed unbiased.

\paragraph{Second-graph configuration.}
PubMed was fixed for its available qualified data, 19,717 nodes, sparse neighborhoods, and small labeled training set, which contrast with arxiv. GraphSAGE uses three layers, hidden width 256, batch normalization/ReLU, dropout 0.5, Adam with learning rate 0.01 and zero weight decay, training fanout $[4,4,4]$, and all 500 epochs. Its 60 official training labels are used for training. The inherited native loader uses 1,000 validation nodes (rows 60--1059), rather than the common PyG 500-node validation configuration. These 1,000 nodes are split into 500 fit and 500 development nodes by a frozen SHA256 namespace and node-ID tiebreaker; every fifth epoch, four fixed fit plans select the earliest maximum-average-accuracy checkpoint. No hyperparameter search or correction training is performed. The 1,000 official test labels were used only after the present design was frozen for its final score. Earlier hyperbolic work used this dataset's test, so we do not claim that PubMed test had never been observed; the present model/configuration was fixed without choosing it by those scores.

The three PubMed base trainings have now completed all 500 epochs and been accepted. Seeds 11, 23, and 37 select epochs 430, 220, and 500, with fit accuracies 74.30\%, 75.05\%, and 73.80\%, respectively. These values document the original selection rule; they are not independent task-quality results. Their allocated GPU times are 100, 87, and 82 seconds, totaling 269 seconds on NVIDIA A800-SXM4-80GB. Together with the 12 reused arxiv base checkpoints, all 15 required weights are available; none is replaced using the new results.

\subsection{Metrics, pairing, and statistical unit}
Primary quality is label accuracy. Deployment evaluation retains accuracy, timing and memory for every model--seed--budget--sampler cell. At the middle budget, the separate diagnostic stream additionally retains per-plan true-label margins, corrections and harms relative to sampled inference, prediction mismatch, and local approximation error under a named coordinate/estimator metric. Sampled and intervention outputs are paired by checkpoint, target ID, and plan. Each seed is first summarized across its prescribed plans; reported mean and sample standard deviation are then across the three training seeds. Repeated node--plan events do not create additional independent training seeds. With three seeds, intervals and sign consistency are descriptive evidence with substantial uncertainty, not high-resolution population estimates.

The four-corner table includes every seed and training regime, including zero, beneficial, nonmonotone, and degenerate cases. An aggregate pooled across architectures or training regimes is labeled as such and does not replace cell-level results. In particular, $\Acc_{11}-\Acc_{00}$ may be negative because the full computation can be less accurate. The interaction in Equation~\eqref{eq:interaction} is computed on the same paired set, without turning it into a fraction of independent causes.

\subsection{Development and independent evaluation}
Historical fit, gate, and confirmation partitions retain their original roles. Fresh sampling plans on previously used labels do not create an independent test. Task A is an offline analysis of existing evidence and is labeled accordingly. New implementation checks and label-free calibration preceded the new scoring. Checkpoints, sampling methods, budgets, intervention locations, metrics, statistical rules, and source hashes were fully fixed before the official test was scored once. No checkpoint replacement, method tuning, budget selection, or selective rerun follows that score. A failure is retained with its execution and data scope; it is not converted into an opportunity to obtain a more favorable result.

\subsection{Resource measurements and reproducibility}
Calibration and final comparisons use NVIDIA A800-SXM4-80GB; historical L40 measurements are a separate hardware stratum. Up to four jobs run on distinct ready GPUs, with serial timing within each device and recorded CPU affinity, memory, transfer and host context. Assigned-device process checks are discrete observations, not proof of continuous host isolation. Timing reports retain input residency, warm-up, synchronization, complete target workload and setup/cache scope. Allocated peaks and reserved allocator-history observations remain distinct; a mixed-budget reserved peak is not a cold minimum capacity.

The new round uses 17,299 allocated GPU-seconds: 269 for three PubMed base trainings, 311 for successful label-free calibration, 13 for the preserved GAT metadata failure, and 16,706 for the 15 final cells. All allocations are closed and released before archive. A scoped tuple/list metadata repair changed no model, sampler, checkpoint or numerical computation. Already accepted qualifications and the SAGE cost lock were inherited without repetition. The public package retains scientific outputs, hardware/usage context and source-version bindings; private scheduler and controller records are excluded. Allocation, worker, scheduler and controller timings have overlapping scopes and are not added; unknown child CPU is not imputed. The final offline synthesis uses 11.375 native CPU-seconds and 14.666 wall-seconds, with no added GPU computation.
